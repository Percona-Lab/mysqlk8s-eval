Second reproduction, plus one more amplifier (same cluster, operator 1.2.0, network volumes):

**Trigger:** `kill -9` of mysqld on the primary 60 s into an `ALTER TABLE` on a 5M-row table, under ~50 tps load.
- Failover took ~157 s. ~13 minutes later the cluster lost write availability again for the rest of the 30 min window (~800 s).
- Events: `Liveness probe failed: in primary partition: false` -> `Container mysql failed liveness probe, will be restarted` on the rejoining member; `Startup probe failed ... Waiting for MySQL ready state` x122 -> `failed startup probe, will be restarted`; `"/opt/percona/healthcheck liveness" timed out after 10s` on another member. In total the mysql containers restarted 5/6/3 times. The group ended with mysql-0 ONLINE PRIMARY, mysql-1 RECOVERING and mysql-2 OFFLINE, and the CR in `error`.

**Additional amplifier: HAProxy liveness depends on the database backends.** `haproxy_liveness_check.sh` runs `select 1` through HAProxy's own frontend (`127.0.0.1:33062`). While no backend passes `haproxy_check_primary.sh` (members restarting or slow), kubelet restarts the HAProxy containers too. All three went to `CrashLoopBackOff` (9-10 restarts each). Even after mysql-0 was ONLINE/PRIMARY and `haproxy_check_primary.sh` returned OK for it when run by hand, clients could not connect until the back-off expired. The probe through `ps-cluster1-haproxy` recovered ~4 min later.

A proxy's liveness should reflect the proxy process itself (e.g. the stats socket / `haproxy -c`), not backend health. Backend health belongs in readiness at most. Otherwise every database incident also restarts the proxy layer and adds CrashLoopBackOff delays on top of the database recovery.
